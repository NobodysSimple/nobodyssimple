# Choose a starting point — remaining pathways
**Editorial and scientific provenance · 9 November 2026**

This record accompanies the expansion of the *Feel steadier* system to **Process something**, **Understand myself**, **Work out what to do**, **Work through something with someone**, **Explore a specific difficulty**, and **I'm not sure**.

## What was built
- **45 distinct, choice-specific educational guidebook drafts** covering every non-*Feel steadier* option (8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 5).
- Each guide includes a recognition explanation, a **possible** pattern rather than a diagnosis, a situation-specific contraindication, and five categories of options: environmental/practical change; a voluntary small practice; cognitive inquiry; acceptance/values; and support/health/safety.
- The guidebook opens in a native dialog and can keep one selected approach alongside the active activity. It never sends or automatically saves reflection notes. The pinned selection is only held in the page session.
- The existing eight *Feel steadier* guidebooks remain intact, as do the 166-topic atlas, direct tools, urgent-support routes, and the original processing-studio exercise.
- The catalogue carries route context in the URL, so choosing a topic or tool from a specific starting point opens that particular guide instead of a generic catch-all.

The count of 45 means 45 editorially distinct entry points, **not** 45 tested clinical interventions. The five categories are organisational prompts, not a recommendation that all five are appropriate in every situation. These guides are educational drafts and have **not** undergone independent clinical review or a controlled outcome evaluation.

Sources support **specific underlying principles or cautions**, not the validity of the content collection as an intervention, nor comparative outcomes across strategies. Editorial wording is deliberately conditional wherever a person's circumstances cannot be known.

## Scientific and epistemic rules
1. **Avoid the Stamp:** no single emotion, action, fantasy, diagnosis, theory or questionnaire result defines a whole person.
2. **Distinguish observation from explanation:** a plausible maintenance cycle is not evidence of an individual cause.
3. **Change external conditions when appropriate:** unsustainable demands, discrimination, poverty, illness, disability or coercion cannot simply be “thought away”.
4. **Separate consensual variation from harm:** chosen relational roles, feelings or identity exploration are not by themselves pathology; restriction of choice and unsafe power are different.
5. **Recognise treatment boundaries:** adapted CBT-, DBT- and ACT-informed practices are not complete, competently delivered therapy protocols. In particular, only specialist-guideline-appropriate care should be positioned as treating a disorder.
6. **Do not equate completion with symptom improvement:** choosing to stop, request support or change the environment can be a successful outcome.

## Remaining review and evaluation

Before describing any guide as clinically reviewed or evidence-based *as an intervention*, commission independent review by relevant clinicians and people with lived experience. Prioritise crisis and coercion, panic and physical health, traumatic experiences, OCD, sleep and fatigue. Check every link and assess culturally situated assumptions. Run observational usability testing on phones, low-stimulation and screen-reader settings, and compare the new guidance with the existing direct tool on comprehension, burden, safety, and real-world transfer. Monitor adverse and neutral experiences, not only completion.

Automated unit, browser and WCAG smoke tests protect software behaviour and some accessibility properties; they are not clinical outcome studies or substitutes for human review.

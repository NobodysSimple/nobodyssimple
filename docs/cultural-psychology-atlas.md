# Cultural Psychology Atlas — homepage feature

## Placement
The interactive Culture Atlas is rendered at the bottom of `#home`, after the existing feedback shortcut. It is not a separate navigation tile or tool. Its markup and behaviour live in `ns-cultural-atlas.mjs`, styling in `ns-cultural-atlas.css`, editorial stories in `ns-cultural-atlas-articles.mjs`, viewport gestures in `ns-cultural-atlas-viewport.mjs`, and geographic services in `ns-cultural-atlas-geography.mjs`.

## Geography
- `data/culture-world-countries.geojson` is a simplified ~1.2 MB vector file derived from the Natural Earth country dataset as distributed by `datasets/geo-countries`; Natural Earth declares its map data public domain (https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). Contains 258 selectable countries, territories, and mapped units, not a claim that there are 258 sovereign nations.
- Maps are illustrative. Statehood and border disputes must not be inferred from mapped line placement.
- The frontend loads country polygons lazily when the user approaches the bottom section.
- On country selection, the frontend requests `https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/{ISO3}/ADM1/` and its simplified geometry. After choosing a state, province or constituent country, an explicit **Explore counties / districts** button loads `ADM2` for that country (outside the US) and filters areas geographically against the selected ADM1 shape. For a US state, county boundaries are instead queried from the US Census TIGERweb **January 2026** Counties layer by two-digit Census state code, avoiding downloading all counties nationwide. Do not assume ADM2 has the same meaning in every country; coverage and vintage vary. Cornwall is available under **United Kingdom → England → Cornwall** if the UK ADM2 service is reachable. Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland and England are on the UK's ADM1 country layer. The US ADM1 layer contains Census states and other first-order units; the US county drilldown uses the separately maintained US Census TIGERweb 2026 dataset and includes state and territory FIPS codes. Civil subdivisions are not a tribal map. geoBoundaries gbOpen data are CC BY 4.0 and/or ODbL with source-specific metadata; do not redistribute licensed boundary files without checking their actual licence and attributions (https://www.geoboundaries.org/api.html). Some countries do not have ADM1 or ADM2 coverage.
- An optional full-screen control expands the map while keeping its zoom, fit and selection controls available. Pan and zoom are independent of selection. SVG viewBox controls support wheel zoom, touch drag, two-finger pinch, keyboard arrow/plus/minus/Home, zoom buttons, and Fit selected area. Touch movement suppresses accidental geographic selection. The world-view button resets both country selection and viewport.
- City/town search is manual and uses the public OpenStreetMap Nominatim service; there is **no autocomplete** or bulk import. Search results are approximate contemporary place names, not a complete city boundary layer. Credit OpenStreetMap contributors and respect their usage limits; for larger production usage, a properly provisioned geocoder is needed.
- A required map layer for the United States uses the US Census TIGERweb AIANNHA 2026 MapServer at https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov/arcgis/rest/services/TIGERweb/AIANNHA/MapServer/47. It loads automatically when the US is selected, queries only the **current zoomed viewport**, up to 500 records per response, and offers a dropdown of visible named Census areas. Users can select a mapped area directly on the map or from the list; there is no opt-in checkbox. It distinguishes recognized reservations, off-reservation trust lands and statistical-designated areas, and is neither an exhaustive map of Indigenous traditional territories nor a statement of jurisdiction. Census geographic coverage may be incomplete or outdated for particular communities; the user must consult the Indigenous nation as primary authority. At large zoom extents, users are asked to zoom in before requesting shapes.
- Each story opens with the features that distinguish the documented practice, then develops its psychological meaning and connects it to peer-reviewed research on related mechanisms. Journal sources are identified by title and citation, with findings and active debates summarized alongside the source link. The atlas now includes additional stories on Turkish coffee, mahadra learning, Xeer Ciise, Gada governance, Ngondo and Nguon rituals, Daida Mwazindika dance, Tai Xòe dance, Haitian Compas, Palenque kuagro, Washi craft, gusle singing, khoomei, Sebeïba, the Khidr Elias feast, Visoko singing, Serer Xooy, Nigerien joking relationships, Paraguayan Guarania, Rwandan Intore, Zambian Kalela, Cuban light-rum apprenticeship, and shared henna, Nowruz, kebaya and pysanka traditions.
- The reader remains usable when third-party shape/geocoding services fail: unavailable layers receive explicit feedback, while country-linked stories are preserved.
- Country, first-level region, second-level area and named city selectors supplement mouse/touch and keyboard map interaction. The selected country, first-level region and second-level area use distinct fills. Indigenous areas are displayed with a separate violet overlay and are never nested under civil states. The world-view button resets selection.
- Browser coverage mocks the subdivision endpoint to avoid depending on third-party service availability in CI. Live external CORS availability and regional naming should also be checked after deployment.

## Editorial coverage
- The atlas contains **94 source-linked short articles across 88 mapped countries and territories**, including several shared traditions that span borders. It still does not have a separate story for every one of the 258 mapped units; uncovered places are labelled clearly instead of being filled with unsupported cultural generalisations.
- The random discovery button chooses a country with a published story, then lands at random at country, first-level-region or second-level-area scale where geographic data is available.
- Every article pairs a primary source on the specific tradition with two or more journal sources on psychological mechanisms. The research section names the study design, describes the findings and identifies live debates such as causal direction, effect size or replication where those matter.
- Country-level stories remain available when a reader selects a smaller area; the panel names the scope of the article. Region-tagged stories appear first when their place name matches.
- Do not portray traditions as universally practiced, imply that a specific culture has an inherent psychological personality, appropriate restricted practices, or call an authored reflective prompt a validated intervention.

## Future work
- Continue adding geographically distributed, community-informed and source-checked contributions to `ns-cultural-atlas-articles.mjs`, with particular attention to Oceania and countries not yet represented. Coverage remains selective; there is no single psychological profile for any country.
- Some countries now have several distinct stories (including India, Korea, Spain, Morocco, Indonesia, Brazil, Mexico and China), and the transnational tango tradition is linked to both Argentina and Uruguay. Do not treat the map as an exhaustive catalogue of cultural identities.
- The initial 32 additions draw on UNESCO heritage entries; the latest 20 also cite official Scottish, Cornish, Welsh, Cherokee Nation, US National Park Service and New Zealand Government material alongside UNESCO records. Article source labels reflect the actual source organisation; heritage status is not evidence of a psychological or clinical outcome.
- Extend geographical coverage beyond first- and second-level administrative boundaries where open, maintained sources make this feasible, particularly city boundaries and higher administrative levels. Do not promise that every country has comparable official subdivisions. Preserve licence requirements, jurisdictional naming and border review.
- Work with Indigenous nations and their data stewards to audit boundaries, design more informative Indigenous territories than a Census statistical overlay and ensure rights, sovereignty, and consent are respected. Do not equate Census datasets with the full extent of homelands.
- Review article perspectives with knowledgeable community members, including the right to correct, contest or withdraw descriptions.
- Make content editorship accessible through the existing staff publication workflow once governance and moderation are agreed.

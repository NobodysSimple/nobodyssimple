**Status:** Evidence-informed editorial build. This is a targeted, non-systematic source review, **not independent clinical peer review or validation** of eight app interventions. The user-facing copy and the individual exercise/guidebook pairs need clinician and lived-experience review before clinical-efficacy claims.

## Scope of this review

Eight pathways covering overwhelm, panic sensations, sensory overload, anger, disconnection/numbing, depletion/fatigue, restlessness/understimulation, and savouring positive experiences.

Each guidebook includes:
1. Plain-English recognition and variations.
2. A *possible*, explicitly non-diagnostic feedback cycle, contributors and change targets.
3. Guidance about when professional assessment or practical support matters.
4. Six change domains: context/environment, behaviour, CBT-informed thought work, DBT-informed distress/emotion skills, ACT-informed flexibility/values, and relational/health resources.
5. Two situation-specific techniques in **each** change domain (12 per topic, 96 unique techniques across the pathway), each with a rationale, 3 concrete steps, a caution and references.
6. Expected signs of useful change that are not limited to symptom reduction.
7. An accessible, user-controlled guidebook overlay that can be opened while continuing the selected tool or worksheet, with optional pinned instructions.

The full guide contains **options**, not a treatment prescription or instruction to complete all methods. Some methods are intentionally unsuitable for some people or situations. This is not a diagnosis or risk assessment.

## Evidence classification

- **Clinical guideline**: NICE recommendations apply to the populations and settings the guideline names. They must not be extrapolated without qualification to all eight self-described experiences.
- **Public health guidance**: NHS health information supports recognition, ordinary practical options and when to seek care; it is not an evaluation of this app.
- **Systematic review**: ACT and savouring reviews support broad intervention principles in studied populations; they do not establish that each drafted micro-skill is effective independently.
- **Specialist practice guidance**: The National Autistic Society and NICE adults-autism guidance support individualized sensory adjustments.
- **Editorial adaptation**: The 96 steps and app interaction are author-composed educational adaptations. Their effectiveness and safety have not been experimentally tested as a package.

## Individual scientific review

### 1. Overwhelm
**Clinical distinction:** Capacity may be exceeded by an unjust workload, inaccessible environment, physical illness or sensory load. It is not always due to cognitive distortions or poor coping.  
**Intervention emphasis:** environmental demands before self-discipline; controllable next steps; cognitive flexibility only around harsh self-judgements; DBT-inspired pauses; ACT-informed values; external help.  
**Contraindications:** do not normalise abusive, unsafe or exploitative situations.  
**Review evidence:** NICE CG142 environmental changes; NHS fatigue; 2024 ACT flexibility meta-analysis; NICE self-harm for structured emotion-regulation interventions (DBT label used cautiously).

### 2. Panic and physical alarm
**Clinical distinction:** recurrent familiar panic is different from new or severe chest pain, collapse or breathing problems. A written distinction must not falsely exclude physical illness.  
**Intervention emphasis:** physical safety first, familiar-response preparation, CBT appraisal and safety-behaviour concepts, optional external orienting, gentle *optional* breathing, ACT willingness only for previously assessed sensations.  
**Contraindications:** no unsupervised intensive interoceptive exposure, prescribed medications changes, extreme cold or compulsory breathwork; red-flag symptoms require urgent care.  
**Review evidence:** NICE CG113, NHS panic disorder.

### 3. Sensory overload
**Clinical distinction:** sensory pain or overstimulation is not itself a thinking error. Autistic and other sensory profiles differ; individual support must be chosen by preference.  
**Intervention emphasis:** lighting, sound, touch and environment adjustments, recovery spaces, low-language supports, sensory aids, autonomy, shame reduction, accessible participation.  
**Contraindications:** no forced habituation or “opposite action” toward painful stimuli.  
**Review evidence:** NICE CG142 recommendations 1.1.8 and 1.3; National Autistic Society sensory processing practice guidance.

### 4. Anger
**Clinical distinction:** anger may reflect real injustice or threat; harmful actions and the legitimacy of the underlying grievance are different questions.  
**Intervention emphasis:** safe pause, reduced confrontation, concrete redistribution of demands, CBT distinction between event and interpretation, DBT-inspired STOP/checking effectiveness, ACT-informed values, non-escalating support.  
**Contraindications:** no forced forgiveness or reconciliation; do not suggest confronting coercive people. Imminent risk to others needs urgent support.  
**Review evidence:** NHS guidance on anger; NICE self-harm support; ACT 2024 review for broader flexibility principles.

### 5. Disconnection / numbing
**Clinical distinction:** shutdown, emotional distance and depersonalisation-like experiences can reflect distinct conditions and contexts; this app does not establish trauma or a dissociative disorder.  
**Intervention emphasis:** low-demand rest, external orientation, predictable routines, accessible communication, cognitive restraint about catastrophic labels, compassion and appropriate review.  
**Contraindications:** no forced traumatic memory processing, extreme body focus or “shock” grounding; new serious symptoms require medical assessment.  
**Review evidence:** NICE NG116 PTSD recognition and appropriate clinical interventions; NICE CG142 accessibility; NHS mindfulness caveats.

### 6. Depletion / fatigue
**Clinical distinction:** fatigue may have physical, occupational, sleep-related or caregiving causes. Post-exertional malaise (PEM) changes suitable management.  
**Intervention emphasis:** load reduction, energy management, realistic pacing, compassionate cognition, ACT-informed values within limits, health review and adjustments.  
**Contraindications:** NICE NG206 explicitly rejects generalized fixed-increment graded exercise therapy for ME/CFS. Do not encourage “push through” strategies in suspected PEM.  
**Review evidence:** NICE NG206, NHS fatigue advice, NICE CG142.

### 7. Restlessness / understimulation
**Clinical distinction:** seeking movement can be an ordinary regulatory preference; new severe agitation may involve medication or illness.  
**Intervention emphasis:** safe sensory or movement choices, environmental permissions, gentle engaging actions, shame reduction, mindfulness-in-motion, impulse flexibility and support.  
**Contraindications:** do not force stillness or movement; do not infer ADHD, mania or another condition from restlessness alone.  
**Review evidence:** NICE CG142, National Autistic Society sensory guidance, NHS wellbeing and mindfulness resources.

### 8. Savouring a positive experience
**Clinical distinction:** joy, gratitude and meaningful pleasure are not problems to treat. Mixed emotions are normal and positive feeling cannot be forced.  
**Intervention emphasis:** one genuine detail, personally meaningful opportunities, memories, cognitive permission for enjoyment, mindful appreciation, optional sharing and valued connection.  
**Contraindications:** no forced gratitude in grief or adversity; abrupt extreme elevation with unusually little sleep and impairment merits clinical assessment.  
**Review evidence:** Cullen et al. (2024) systematic review of savouring in clinical populations; NHS Five Ways to Wellbeing; ACT psychological flexibility review.

## Therapy-label integrity

- **CBT-informed** = examination of appraisals, predictions, behaviour and contextual evidence, not delivery of the full NICE-endorsed protocol.
- **DBT-informed** = optional, adapted pause, sensory self-soothing, facts-versus-urge and distress tolerance, not an independently delivered course of DBT.
- **ACT-informed** = willingness, defusion, values and committed action *within real constraints*, not a claim that acceptance cures physical illness or replaces systemic change.
- **Environmental and structural** = adjustments, accessibility, redistribution of demands and medical care can be primary targets, not alternatives of lesser importance.

## UI and usability implementation review

- A single compact “Open the full guidebook” control is shown beside each Feel Steadier exercise.
- A responsive native \`dialog\` displays all six guide sections on demand, with six collapsed/expandable skill groups.
- A selected method can be pinned beside the current tool, and removed or replaced.
- Contextual \`#steady-tool/<guide>/<tool>\` routes preserve the connection without changing the original tool identifier.
- Direct visits to reused tools provide a short guide-selector rather than assuming the user’s issue.
- No notes are auto-saved or uploaded by the guidebook; selected *method IDs only* are retained for the current JS session.
- Browser CI covers desktop and mobile Chromium, navigation, focus return, horizontal overflow and automated WCAG violations in relevant components.
- **Not yet accomplished:** moderated usability studies, screen-reader testing by human participants, expert clinical sign-off, and individual efficacy trials.

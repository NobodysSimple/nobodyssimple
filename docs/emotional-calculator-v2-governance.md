# Emotional Calculator v2: release and review register

Status: RESEARCH PROTOTYPE. This is NOT a validated psychological assessment, diagnosis, triage or treatment service.

## Implemented
- The homepage is unchanged. The console begins with one plain-language experience entry, four example starters, and optional browse access to the existing 166 topics.
- Candidate explanations compete. Different follow-up questions collect support, contradictions, exceptions and relevant circumstances instead of presupposing the user knows the cause.
- A user can reject an explanation, consider alternatives and review a small real-world outcome without receiving a diagnostic probability.
- The ten educational sections remain accessible, grouped into Recognise, Understand and Choose a next step.
- Selected circumstances, personal variables and the revisable formulation remain in transient JS module memory; no account sync.
- Cross-cutting red flags are checked independently of the initial topic and conservative support messages are shown.
- Structured outcome review replaces the previous answer-counting response in the exercise route.

## Important limits
- Most of the 166 topic pages still rely on broad shared educational templates. The catalogue is not 166 independently clinically reviewed interventions.
- The reasoning questions and priorities are expert-designed educational heuristics; they are not psychometrically calibrated or clinically validated.
- Candidate rank should not be interpreted as a diagnosis, probability, causality or a definitive recommendation.
- The small citation registry links selected reputable material but the complete claim-to-evidence mapping and review log are not yet established.
- The app cannot reliably interpret all unfamiliar phrasings or independently assess risk and contraindications.

## Hazard register for clinical review
1. False reassurance if harmful circumstances are described in unfamiliar language.
2. False alarms when text includes negation, speculation or quoted material.
3. Coercion or actual mistreatment incorrectly treated as interpersonal misinterpretation or avoidant behaviour.
4. Severe physical symptoms interpreted as anxiety or insomnia; do not delay medical assessment.
5. Possible psychosis, mania, eating disorders or substance withdrawal routed to inappropriate self-help.
6. Intrusive harm thoughts incorrectly assumed to represent intent, or actual intent incorrectly dismissed.
7. Misleading impression that arrows in the personal map establish causal relationships.
8. Bias across disability, neurodivergence, language, culture and socioeconomic circumstances.
9. Sensitive personal data retention if future account sync is introduced without a lawful basis and explicit user controls.
10. Content drift and broken external resources; no source may be called individually validated without review.

## Prerequisites before clinical or 'first in the world' claims
- Independent clinical risk/hazard review with adversarial vignettes, sign-off and contraindication rules.
- Human review of each major problem pathway and relevant intervention; record provenance, limitations, author, reviewer and review date.
- Empirical comparative evaluation of safety, problem identification, formulation relevance, user comprehension and actionability.
- Cross-browser, mobile, keyboard and screen-reader testing.
- Proper DPIA, UK GDPR documentation, and applicable medical software or NHS safety standards if intended use changes.
- A mechanism ontology connecting specific signs, hypotheses, context, evidence, interventions and outcomes, not merely inherited templates.

## Smoke cases
- "My partner monitors my phone": coercion and safety-first.
- "I don't feel safe at home": safety-first.
- "I wake up gasping": physical-health assessment cue.
- "My boss bullies me": external/institutional issue.
- "I think everyone is watching me": cautious interpretation without automatic diagnosis.
- "I think of harming someone": distinguish intrusive harm thoughts from actual intent; prioritise support.
- "I am NOT suicidal": not automatically flagged as positive suicidal ideation.
- "I enjoy being submissive": consensual preference is not a disorder.
- "I keep checking my messages": distinguish habit, relationship uncertainty, work demands, obsessional checking and coercion.
- "I put off university work": distinguish fear of mistakes, ambiguous steps, capacity and unreasonable demands.
